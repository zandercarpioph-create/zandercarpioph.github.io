Dengue surveillance, Baguio City

R · QGIS · Spatiotemporal analysis

Barangay-level dengue surveillance across 127 barangays and 23 reporting weeks, joined on PSGC codes and mapped in QGIS. The series resolves a sharp outbreak peaking in the week of 9 to 15 January, decaying from 1,778 reported cases to fewer than 70 within six weeks, then holding at a low baseline for the rest of the year.

Cleaning mattered more than modelling. Two things in this dataset will quietly produce wrong answers: the files are labelled as incidence per 1,000 but hold integer case counts, and several barangay names contain commas, so any naive comma split silently undercounts. My first cross-check reported 1,534 cases for a week whose true total was 1,778. I only caught it because the two routes disagreed.

Surveillance data obtained from a health office and not redistributed. Only derived aggregates are published here, and barangay names are withheld. The finding is the steepness of the gradient, which does not require naming which communities carry it.

Compartmental epidemic models

R · deSolve · Shiny · Live in browser

SIR and SEIR systems with mass-action incidence, integrated with deSolve in R and wrapped in Shiny so the parameters can be moved and the epidemic curve re-solved live.

The version on this page is the same mathematics reimplemented from scratch in JavaScript with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta integrator. Building it twice, in two languages, is the point: when both produce the same curve, the dynamics belong to the model rather than to the solver.

Switch to SEIR and watch what a latent compartment does. The same transmission pressure yields a later, lower, broader peak once people spend time exposed but not yet infectious.

Cholera transmission with an environmental reservoir

R · MATLAB · Shiny

An SEIRB model in which cholera spreads two ways at once: directly between people, and indirectly through a reservoir of bacteria in contaminated water. The bacterial compartment is what makes the model worth building, because it is the one an intervention can actually act on.

Implemented as a 797-line Shiny application in R with fourteen tunable parameters, and independently again in MATLAB. Two implementations of one system is not duplicated effort, it is the cheapest way to find out that the solver is not inventing the dynamics.

Kaprekar triples, undergraduate thesis

Number theory · PARI/GP · LaTeX

Extending Iannucci and Foster's 2005 work on Kaprekar triples: integers whose cube splits into three blocks that sum back to the original number. 8 is the smallest, because 83 = 512 splits as 5, 1 and 2.

The central result turns an infinite search into a finite one by proving every triple is a root of a congruence. That bounds the count at four for each power of ten and rules out eleven residue classes entirely.

The project runs on a rule I set myself: no result counts until two independently written programs agree. It has earned its keep twice. It caught a counting error in my own code that had understated a maximum by a third, and the chart here, written fresh in R, reproduces the counts my PARI/GP work had already established.

Kuramoto synchronisation

Python · Jupyter · R

Coupled-oscillator dynamics: a population of oscillators, each with its own natural frequency, pulled toward one another by a coupling term. Below a critical coupling they drift incoherently. Above it they lock into a collective rhythm, and the transition is sharp.

Originally studied in a Python notebook; the sweep shown here was recomputed in R so that the whole site regenerates from one script.

About

A short version

I am a mathematics undergraduate at UP Baguio, supported by a DOST-SEI scholarship and graduating in 2027. My coursework has run from real and complex analysis through operations research. My applied work has mostly been epidemiological: compartmental models of cholera and dengue, and the surveillance data that comes with them.

Interning at the Philippine Statistics Authority taught me the part of quantitative work that textbooks leave out. Data arrives inconsistently labelled, joins fail on encoding, and the interesting question is usually whether the number in front of you means what its column header claims. The dengue files on this page are labelled as rates and contain counts. That is not an unusual case, it is the normal one.

Nothing counts as a result until two independently written programs agree on it.

My thesis pushed that instinct further. Working on Kaprekar triples I adopted that rule, and it caught a real error in my own work that had gone unnoticed for months. It is a slow way to work and I would not give it up, because the alternative is confidently shipping a number that happens to be wrong.

I am looking for actuarial and data science roles where that combination is useful: enough mathematics to know what a model assumes, and enough scepticism to check whether the data supports it.
